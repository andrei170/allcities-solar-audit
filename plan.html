Marketing Action Plan

AllCities Solar Power+ — What We Would Actually Do

Prepared for Paul Comiskey · 30 August 2026 · AG1 Consulting
The strategy in one paragraph

You complete about 4.5 jobs a month, roughly 50 a year, averaging $41,000, and 90% of it comes from word of mouth. Word of mouth is the best lead source there is — and it is also the reason you cannot grow. You cannot buy more of it, you cannot turn it up in a quiet month, and it does not scale to four crews.

And the target is smaller than it sounds. You told us $2 million needs only two more people, and that it is “not that big of a jump.” So this is not about doubling overnight — it is about producing enough reliable work to justify two hires.

The plan is to add a second engine alongside it, in the one window where all four of the out-of-state operators you named have failed and left roughly 2,000 of their customers stranded in your counties. Not to replace what works. To stop it being the only thing you have.

Phase One — Fix What Makes Spending Impossible
Before a single dollar of advertising

The Foundation

Weeks 1–3 · one-off, not a monthly fee
  • A qualifying enquiry form on every page. Bill size, roof age, property type, county. There is currently no form anywhere on your site, including Contact Us — so the 6–8 weak leads a month arrive with nothing attached and every one costs you a phone call to qualify.
  • A bill upload. Your contact page already asks for annual usage. Right now there is nowhere to send it.
  • Conversion tracking and call tracking. Without these, advertising is bought blind and the platform optimises toward clicks because clicks are all it can see.
  • Rewrite the Government Incentives page. It is currently live with unedited Squarespace placeholder text — “It all begins with an idea” — on the single subject every customer in Washington is confused about.
  • An orphaned-system landing page. The destination for the campaign below.
  • County pages for Yakima, Kittitas, Klickitat, Benton and Franklin.

Why this is not optional: every dollar of advertising ends up pointing at a page. Spending before this is done means paying for visitors who have no way to become customers, and no way to be counted.

Phase Two — Four Campaigns, In This Order
1

The customers Solgen and the others left behind

“Your installer went out of business. We are still here.” By your own count, Solgen, Purelight, Lumio, Freedom Solar and Revolution Solar left around 2,000 customers in your area — and you have made about 15 of them whole.

Why first: the customers already exist and already have a fault. No tax credit is involved, so January changed nothing here. You already do this work reactively when a bank or the utility calls you — this makes it something you win on purpose.
2

Commercial & Agricultural

Orchards, vineyards, hop yards, packing houses, cold storage and irrigation across the Yakima Valley. Large, year-round electricity users who buy on payback arithmetic.

Why second: Section 48E still runs to 2032 for commercial systems, so this half of the market did not change in January. You are a licensed electrical and general contractor, which is what a packing shed actually needs. Highest value per job.
3

Power Storage & Off-Grid

Backup power as outages increase, and the fully off-grid builds you described — the sixty-acre plot in the foothills where bringing power in is impossible or uneconomic, and the customer never needs a utility again.

Why third: it never depended on a tax credit, it is the upsell to everyone from campaign one, and the hybrid inverter and lithium-iron-phosphate work is the specialism most solar firms cannot do at all.
4

Wind, from October

Three brands, installing from October, positioned on the reliability story — you left the category in 2017 because the equipment could not survive your climate, and you have come back now it can.

Why fourth: nobody else in central Washington can run this campaign. Subject to the 25-foot limit inside city limits, so it aims at rural and acreage properties — the same off-grid customers as campaign three.
And notice what is not on that list

Residential solar to new customers on a cash purchase. That is the one the credit change broke, and it is the one every other agency will try to sell you. You expect a Washington state residential rebate in 2027. That is when this lane comes back — and by then everything above is running and measured.

Phase Three — The Two Dates You Already Have
This is why starting now matters

October wind. January rebrand.

Built through autumn, live for both
  • October 2026 — wind turbines. Three brands, and a campaign nobody else in central Washington can run. Conditional on you confirming the 25-foot city limit and county permitting, which is the constraint that shapes who it can be sold to.
  • January 2027 — the rebrand to All Cities, Solar, Wind and Power. A rebrand is the moment search rankings, reviews and a Google profile are most at risk and most improvable. Doing it with tracking already in place means you can prove what it did rather than hope.

And your own September campaign sits alongside this, not instead of it. The animation and character concept is brand work and it should run — what is missing underneath it is the form and the tracking, without which you will not know whether it worked. The reason to start now rather than January: the foundation takes three weeks, the campaigns need four to six weeks of data before they are worth judging, and both of your dates land inside that window. Starting in January means the rebrand launches into the same setup you have today.
