So this document is built as an argument, not a brochure

You asked for three things and we ran out of time on all of them: the testimonials and results, an action plan, and whether you can afford it. They are the three sections below, in that order.

Every number in here is yours, taken from what you told us on Friday. Where we have made an assumption we have said so, and where we cannot promise something we have said that too.

First — Two Corrections To What We Showed You
We had two things wrong and you should hear it from us

Ownership, and why you stopped doing wind

1. We described the business as family-owned since 1979. That came from your Our Story page, which is written in the previous owner's voice. In fact the Mills family founded it in 1979, you started running it in 2017 and bought it in October 2024. That matters for marketing, because “47 years serving Yakima” is true of the business and needs careful wording, whereas “the same company since 1979, now owned by the man who ran it for seven years” is both true and stronger.

2. We wrote that you stopped installing wind because of code limitations. Your own account is different and more useful: the early bladed turbines were a warranty nightmare, with metal fatigue and failure in a climate with your extremes. Code is a separate constraint — 25 feet inside city limits. That is a much better story: you did not avoid wind, you refused to sell equipment that broke.

We would rather correct ourselves than have you find it later.

2. The Action Plan
Phase one — before any advertising

Make the site capable of catching what it already gets

You get six to eight enquiries a month from the website, and you told us half of them are people trying to sell you something. So the real number is three or four. Before spending on traffic, the page has to be able to hold what already arrives.

A qualifying form on every page (ownership, bill size, roof age, county) · a bill upload, because your contact page already asks for annual usage and gives nowhere to send it · conversion and call tracking · the Government Incentives page rewritten, which is still live with Squarespace placeholder text · county pages for Yakima, Kittitas, Klickitat, Benton and Franklin.

Three weeks. One-off. Yours to keep whether or not you run a campaign with us.

Sequenced by what is provable soonest

The customers Solgen and the others left behind

You told us Solgen went bankrupt, came back as Purelight after a $250,000 fine, and was gone within a year — and that between them, Solgen, Lumio, Freedom Solar and Revolution Solar left roughly 2,000 customers in your area. You have made about 15 of them whole.

That is 15 out of 2,000. Those people own solar, have a fault, have no warranty and nobody to call. No tax credit is involved, so January changed nothing about this line — and nobody is advertising to them.

Commercial & agricultural

You said it yourself: the residential federal incentive went, the commercial one did not. Orchards, packing houses, cold storage and irrigation across the valley.

Highest job values, an incentive that still applies, and you are a licensed electrical and general contractor, so nothing is subbed out.

Power storage & off-grid

Backup power as outages increase, and the fully off-grid builds — the sixty-acre plot in the foothills where bringing power in is impossible or uneconomic.

Never depended on a tax credit. And it is the natural upsell to every customer from campaign one, who already has panels and no storage.

Three brands, installing from October. Positioned on the reliability story — you left the category when the equipment could not survive your climate, and you have come back now it can.

Nobody else in central Washington can run this campaign. Subject to the 25-foot city limit, so it is aimed at rural and acreage properties.
What we are not proposing, and why

New residential solar on a cash purchase. That is the lane the federal change broke and it is the one every other agency will pitch you. If the Washington state rebate you expect arrives in 2027, that is when it comes back — and by then everything above is running and measured.

We are also not proposing to replace the September campaign you have already planned. You have an animation and character concept in development. That is brand work and it should keep running — what is missing underneath it is the tracking and the form, without which you will never know whether it worked.

3. “I Just Want To Make Sure We Can Afford It”
“I want to do this research, become knowledgeable, think it through, ask my questions, and start the process — and just want to make sure we can afford it.” Paul, 29 August
The honest way to answer that is break-even, not projections

How much work does this have to produce before it costs you nothing?

You told us your net margin is 7–10% and your average job over the last 36 months is $41,000. So the only question that matters is how many jobs pay for the marketing.

Average job value (your figure)
$41,000
Net margin (your figure, taken at the low end)
7%
Net profit from one average job
$2,870
Growth plan, all in, per month
$4,000
Jobs per month to break even
1.4

At your 10% margin it is one job a month. At the low end of your own range it is just under one and a half. You currently complete about 4.5 jobs a month and want to reach nine.

And on the Starter tier it is 0.9 jobs a month at 7% — under one average job.

That is the whole affordability answer. Not a forecast of what we will produce, but the number the work has to beat before it costs you anything. You told us you need $20,000 a month just to keep the lights on; this sits alongside that as a variable cost with a break-even you can check yourself every month.

The other affordability question, which nobody asks

Two people. Not eleven.

You said reaching $2 million would need just two more people, and that it is “not that big of a jump.” That is a $500,000 revenue increase from two hires.

So the marketing does not have to deliver twenty jobs a month. It has to reliably deliver enough to justify two hires and keep them busy. That is a far smaller and far more believable target than doubling, and it is the one we would build toward first.

And it pays us nothing
