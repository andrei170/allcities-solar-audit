Paul Comiskey — Onboarding Call

AllCities Solar Power+ · Wednesday 2 September 2026, 1:00pm PST · 9:00pm BST
Settle this before anything else

Is the money in? As of 30 August the deposit had not been paid. An onboarding call where payment is still open is a sales call wearing different clothes — and if you run it as onboarding without checking, you will hand over four weeks of scope for free.

If it has cleared: run the agenda below as written.

If it has not: do not open with it and do not pretend it does not matter. Run the first ten minutes as planned, then say plainly — “before I put the team on this, let me get the first payment sorted so we can start Monday.” Then carry on. Asking once, calmly, mid-call, works. Asking three times does not.

The Deal, In One Line Each
What he agreed to on 30 August
  • Phase one — $2,500 one-off. Website overhaul: enquiry form, bill upload, conversion and call tracking, rewritten incentives page, county pages.
  • Phase two — Starter, $2,500/month. $1,250 management, $1,250 ad spend. One to two campaigns to begin.
  • Reviewed at 90 days before deciding whether to move to the $4,000 tier.
  • No long contract, no lock-in.

The break-even he accepted: 0.9 jobs a month. $41,000 average job at 7% net is $2,870 profit; the package is $2,500. He already does about 4.5 jobs a month. If he wobbles on price, that is the number to go back to — and it uses his own figures at the worst end of his own margin range.

Running Order — Keep It To Forty Minutes
Minutes 0–5

Confirm the order of work, out loud

Commercial and agricultural first. Storage second. Electrical third. Stranded systems last.

Say why it is in that order, in his words, not yours: he was the one who moved stranded systems to the back, because it is low-margin service work needing roughly two people at 60% of their time. Repeating his own reasoning back to him is the fastest trust-builder available today — it proves the last call was listened to rather than sold at.

Minutes 5–20

Collect access — the only part that must not be skipped

Nothing in week one happens without these. Work down the checklist below on the call and get them in the chat or by email while he is still on the line. “I’ll send it later” becomes next Tuesday.

Tracking goes on first, before any build. Say that plainly — it is why the 90-day review means anything.

Minutes 20–30

The four questions only he can answer

  • Which counties matter most? Yakima, Kittitas, Klickitat, Benton, Franklin. Campaign one cannot be spread across all five on $1,250 of spend. Make him choose two.
  • What qualifies an enquiry? He knows which answers separate a real commercial job from a tyre-kicker. The form is built from his answer, not ours.
  • Who answers the phone and the form? Get the name and the email today — they need the team briefing page and an invite.
  • What did Ashley leave behind? Logins, drafts, and the September animation work in progress.
Minutes 30–38

Set the reporting expectation now, not at day 45

Reporting is against cost per booked job, not clicks. Say it on this call so it is never a surprise.

And say the uncomfortable thing while goodwill is high: campaigns need four to six weeks of data before they are worth judging, which is why the review is at ninety days. If he expects leads in week two, the relationship is damaged by week three.

Minutes 38–40

His questions, and one date

He told you he would have questions once he had thought it through. Leave real room for them — this is the call he was told to bring them to.

End with a specific next date, not “we’ll be in touch”. Week-one check-in, in the diary, before you hang up.

Three Things To Handle Carefully
Each of these can quietly go wrong

1. The wind turbines. He is re-entering wind in October and rebranding to “All Cities, Solar, Wind and Power” in January. But he rejected wind in 2007 over county code height limits, and nobody has confirmed those limits changed. Do not build marketing on wind until he confirms permitting — that is his job to check, and it needs saying today rather than in November.

2. Stranded systems stay in the drawer. It is the most exciting line commercially and it is the one he explicitly held back. If you reopen it because it is easy, you are overriding the customer on his own operations — and he will notice.

3. Anything about tax is not ours to advise on. Section 48E, storage treatment, the Washington rebate. Give context, then point at his CPA. It is already disclaimed on the agreed page — keep the same line verbally.

Facts Worth Having In Front Of You
If the conversation drifts, these anchor it
  • ~$1.5M revenue, ~50 jobs a year, $41,000 average job, 7–10% net. All his figures.
  • 90% word-of-mouth, and only 6–8 poor website leads a month.
  • Goal: 100+ jobs, $3M+, scaling two crews to four, 9 staff to 20. But the real 90-day target is $2M, which he said needs only two more people.
  • Both competitors he named are gone. Solgen/Purelight ceased all Washington operations 13 January 2026 with $50–100M of liabilities. Lumio went Chapter 11 then Chapter 7.
  • Ellensburg Solar is the live competitor — markets well, tracks properly, a page per town, but weak enough after the sale that their customers ring Paul for help.
  • Pacific Power is around 15¢/kWh and seeking roughly 14% more. A rising bill is the argument that replaces the dead residential credit.
  • Serving Yakima since 1979; licensed electrical AND general contractor. Nothing subbed out — that is the positioning nobody else can copy.

The agreed position is stability, not size. He was explicit that he does not want to be the biggest or the baddest. “We will still be here when your system needs looking at.”
