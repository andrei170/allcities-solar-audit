The Constraint That Decides Everything
Starter package — what the budget actually buys

At $1,250 of spend you can run two campaigns properly or four campaigns badly. Split four ways, each gets roughly $300 a month, and none of them ever gathers enough data to learn anything.

So the order matters far more than the list. Everything below is about which two go first and why.

Campaign One — Commercial And Agricultural
60 to 70% of the budget. This is the one we would open on.

Section 48E runs to 2032. The deadline pressure that is about to hit residential does not apply here, which means you are not competing with panic and you are not discounting against a clock.

Bigger jobs, fewer firms bidding, and your electrical licence is a genuine qualifier. Most residential solar outfits cannot touch commercial work. You can, and you have been able to since 1979.

Who we would target: farms, orchards, wineries, packing houses and light industrial across the Valley. Agricultural operations are the obvious ones — irrigation pumps run on power, power is their biggest controllable cost, and the credit is still there.

Campaign Two — Power Storage
30 to 40% of the budget, and the reason people buy it is not what you think

Storage keeps the full federal credit. That alone makes it the strongest residential offer you have left.

But in your market the buying reason is not money at all. It is the well. No power means no water — for the house, for the livestock, for the stock tank. That is a fear-of-loss sale, not a savings sale, and it converts completely differently. Nobody calculates a payback period on running water.

The same applies to medical need. Anybody on oxygen, refrigerated medication or a powered bed is not buying an energy product, they are buying certainty.

Nobody sells storage this way in your market. They all sell it on bill savings, which is the weakest argument available.

Campaigns Three And Four — And Why They Wait
Both are good. Neither is first.

Electrical work keeps crews busy and every panel upgrade is a future solar lead. It is genuinely valuable and it is the smartest long game in your business. But it does not need paid traffic to fill a diary yet, and spending against it now would take budget from the two campaigns that open new ground.

Orphan system rescue is the one everybody would tell you to run, and we would not. Solgen and Lumio going under left a very large number of homeowners in your region with nobody to call — on our call we put that somewhere between twenty-five and fifty thousand.

You told us yourself why it does not work yet: service is low margin, and doing it properly needs two people at around sixty percent of their time. Advertising into that before the team exists would create a problem rather than solve one. You would be buying obligations, not customers.

It stays on the list. When there are two people who can run it, it becomes one of the best assets you have — because those homeowners already own systems, already need service, and already know their original installer is gone.

Your Two Ideas — Both Good, Both Phase Two
Willy Wind and the live production meter

The live production meter is the better of the two and it is genuinely unusual. A real number from a real system, updating, in public. That is proof rather than a claim, and absolutely nobody in your market is doing anything like it. Every competitor is making assertions about output. You would be publishing yours.

Willy Wind is a brand asset and it is a fair instinct. Solar advertising is relentlessly serious and being the memorable one in a boring category is a real advantage over years.

Both build brand, and brand needs traffic pointed at it first. At $1,250 a month the first job is finding out which of the two campaigns actually pays. Once that is known, these are where the next money goes.

Why The Timing Actually Matters
Three clocks, none of which we control

Wind season returns in October. That is the part of your business nobody else in the Valley can do at all, and it has a season. Campaigns need a few weeks to get out of the learning phase before they are worth judging, which means starting in October is already starting late.

Ellensburg Solar is already doing this. You told us their post-sale reputation is poor enough that their own customers ring you for help — but their digital marketing is good. Tracking, town-specific pages, the lot. Every month that stays true, they take more of the orphaned Solgen and Lumio customers than you do, and they are not better than you at the work.

And the residential credit position is changing. The maths a homeowner runs is not the maths they ran last year. The first company in Yakima to explain the new position clearly and honestly wins, and that window closes as soon as somebody else does it.
