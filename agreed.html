Agreed plan — for review before onboarding

AllCities Solar Power+ — What We Agreed

Paul Comiskey · 30 August 2026 · AG1 Consulting

The decision, in writing

Phase one — foundation: $2,500, one-off. Website overhaul: enquiry form, bill upload, conversion and call tracking, a rewritten incentives page, and county pages.

Phase two — Starter package: $2,500 a month. $1,250 management, $1,250 ad spend. One to two campaigns to begin.

Reviewed at 90 days before deciding whether to scale to the $4,000 tier, which carries $2,000 of management, $2,000 of spend and all four campaigns.

No long contract and no lock-in. This page is a summary for your review, not a contract; final terms are confirmed separately in writing.

Why We Start Where We Start

You changed the running order on our call and you were right to. We had put the stranded-customer work first because it is the easiest to win. You pointed out it is low-margin service work that would need roughly two people at 60% of their time to run efficiently — so winning more of it before you have that team makes your business worse, not better. It has moved to last.

1

Commercial & Agricultural

Orchards, vineyards, hop yards, packing houses, cold storage and irrigation across the valley. Large, year-round users who buy on payback arithmetic.

First because: highest job value, the Section 48E credit runs to 2032, and being a licensed electrical and general contractor means nothing is subbed out.
2

Power Storage & Backup

Backup for homes on wells, properties with medical equipment, and the fully off-grid builds where bringing utility power in is impossible or uneconomic.

Second because: it keeps the full federal credit, it sells on consequences rather than payback, and the hybrid inverter and lithium-iron-phosphate work is a specialism most solar firms cannot do at all.
3

Electrical Work

Panel upgrades, service work, EV charging. The line most solar companies have to subcontract.

Third because: it keeps crews busy between installs, no solar policy change can touch it, and every panel upgrade is a solar conversation eighteen months later.
4

Stranded-System Rescue — held back deliberately

The customers left behind by Solgen, Purelight, Lumio, Freedom Solar and Revolution Solar.

Last because you said so: low margin, and it needs a dedicated service team before volume helps. The demand is not going anywhere — those people already find you one at a time through banks and the utility. We turn it on when the team exists to absorb it.
The Positioning

Stability, against a market that has been let down

Your competitor situation is unusual. The aggressive out-of-state operators failed. What remains locally — Ellensburg Solar in particular — markets well, with tracking and a page for every town, but has a post-sale reputation weak enough that their customers ring you for help.

So the position is not “we are bigger”. You were explicit that you do not want to advertise being the biggest or the baddest — you want to be the best and the most memorable. The line that does that work is stability:

“We will still be here when your system needs looking at.”

Backed by three things nobody else in the market can claim together: a company serving Yakima since 1979, a licensed electrical contractor rather than a sales organisation, and wind turbines from October — which no competitor here offers at all.

Your two creative ideas — both worth building

The animated characters, including Willy Wind. Memorable, ownable, and completely unlike the identikit solar advertising in this market. That is brand work and it should run alongside the campaigns, not instead of them.

The real-time production meter on a digital reader board. This is the stronger of the two commercially — live kilowatt-hours from a real system, on the busiest street in Yakima County, is proof rather than a claim. It is also the single best use of the signage you already want.

The First Four Weeks
Week 1

Onboarding, access to the site, ad accounts and analytics. Tracking and call tracking installed first so there is a baseline.

Week 2

The enquiry form and bill upload built, with the qualifying questions agreed with you — you know which answers matter.

Week 3

Incentives page rewritten, county pages, and the commercial landing page. Everything tested end to end.

Week 4

Campaign one live. Reporting set up against cost per booked job rather than clicks.

Working estimates, not contractual dates — timing depends on how quickly access and content come back from your side. Campaigns need four to six weeks of data before they are worth judging, which is why the review is at ninety days rather than thirty.
