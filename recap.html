the day you said yes

You agreed the phased rollout on Sunday 30 August. This page is everything that led to it, on one screen.

Two calls, back to back on the Saturday and Sunday. The first was the numbers and the website. The second was the market and the decision.

It has been a couple of weeks, so rather than make you remember any of it, here it is.

All figures on this page are yours, as you gave them to us on those calls
Call One — Saturday 29 August
The business, the goal, and why the website was the blocker
  • Where you are. Around $1.5M a year, roughly 50 jobs, average job about $41,000, net margin 7–10%. Ninety per cent of it word of mouth.
  • Where you want to be. Double it. $3M+, 100+ jobs a year, two crews to four, nine people to twenty — specialising across solar, wind and storage so fixed costs sit further from revenue.
  • What is in the way. The website scored 40/100. No proper enquiry form, no conversion tracking, no county pages, and an incentives page still carrying unedited Squarespace placeholder text. You were getting six to eight poor leads a month from it.
  • What is coming. Wind re-entry in October 2026, and the rebrand to All Cities Solar, Wind and Power in January 2027.
  • The one free thing. No proper signage at 2014 S 1st St — roughly 35,000 cars a day going past.
The arithmetic we walked through, on your own numbers

$40–80 per qualified lead. Your close rate estimate of 25–30%. Your $41,000 average job.

Run deliberately pessimistically — $1,250 of spend, 20 leads, and a 15% close rather than your 25–30% — that is 3 customers, about $123,000 of revenue, and roughly $12,300 of net at a 10% margin.

These are illustrative figures on your assumptions, not a forecast. The point of the exercise was only to show the shape holds even when you halve the close rate.

Call Two — Sunday 30 August
The market gap, and the order we do things in
  • The gap. Solgen and Lumio went under and left somewhere between 25,000 and 50,000 homeowners in the region with nobody to call. Your main competitor markets well but has a poor reputation after the sale — which is why their customers already ring you.
  • What nobody can copy. Serving Yakima since 1979. Licensed electrical and general contractor. Wind capability. Nothing subbed out.
  • Campaign order, agreed. Commercial and agricultural first — Section 48E runs to 2032. Then power storage, which keeps the full federal credit and matters to anyone on a well. Then electrical work, because every panel upgrade is a future solar job. Orphan system rescue parked — you were right that the margin is too thin without two people on it at 60% of their time.
  • Your two ideas, not ours. The superhero theme with Willy Wind, and a live kWh production meter on a digital billboard showing a real system's output. Both are still on the table and both are better than anything in this market.
