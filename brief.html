Who AG1 Are, And What They Were Asked To Do

AG1 Consulting is a marketing agency working mostly with home-services and trades businesses in the US and UK. Paul was introduced through a call in August, and over two conversations on 29 and 30 August the work was scoped and agreed.

The brief is narrow and deliberate. Not a rebrand, not a replacement for anything the company already does, and not more residential solar leads — that market changed when the federal residential credit ended. The job is to make the website capable of capturing enquiries, make everything measurable, and then run a small number of campaigns aimed at the work that is still worth having.

What Was Found On The Website
Not criticism of anyone — context for what is being fixed

Ashley built and ran the website and social media for two and a half years and has been on maternity leave since the end of June. Everything below is a consequence of nobody having picked that work up, not of it having been done badly.

  • No enquiry form on any page, including Contact Us. The contact page asks people to send their annual usage, address and account name — and gives them nowhere to put it.
  • The Government Incentives page is live with unedited Squarespace placeholder text — “It all begins with an idea. Maybe you want to launch a business…” — on the one subject every customer is currently confused about.
  • Google Analytics is installed and working (property G-7DWRQN9VKJ), so visits are counted.
  • But there is no conversion tracking and no call tracking, so there is no way to know which visits turned into work, or where a phone call came from.
  • No page for any individual county — Yakima, Kittitas, Klickitat, Benton and Franklin are named but none has a page of its own.
The Market, And Why The Timing Matters
The competitive picture as discussed on the calls

The aggressive out-of-state operators have failed. Paul named four — Solgen (which returned as Purelight after a $250,000 fine and closed within a year), Lumio, Freedom Solar and Revolution Solar. Between them they left roughly 2,000 customers in this area with systems and no support. AllCities has made about 15 of them whole so far.

The main remaining local competitor is Ellensburg Solar, around 35 miles away. They advertise properly — Google Ads, call tracking, and a separate page for Yakima, Selah, Sunnyside, Toppenish and most of the surrounding towns. Their weakness is after the sale, to the point that their customers ring AllCities for help.

So the position is not that AllCities is bigger. Paul was explicit that he does not want to advertise being the biggest — he wants the company to be the best and the most memorable. The line the marketing is built around is stability: “we will still be here when your system needs looking at.”

What Is Being Built, And In What Order
Phase one · first three weeks

The foundation

An enquiry form on every page asking a few qualifying questions, a way to upload a power bill, conversion and call tracking, the incentives page rewritten properly, and a page for each county. One-off piece of work. Nothing is advertised until this exists, because spending on traffic that lands on a page with no form wastes the money and cannot be measured.

Campaign 1

Commercial & agricultural

Orchards, packing houses, cold storage, irrigation. Highest job values, and the federal commercial incentive still applies where the residential one does not.

Campaign 2

Power storage & backup

Homes on wells, properties with medical equipment, and off-grid builds. Sold on what happens when the power goes out rather than on payback.

Campaign 3

Electrical work

Panel upgrades, service work, EV charging. Keeps crews busy between installs and is unaffected by any change in solar policy.

Held back

Rescuing stranded systems

Deliberately last. Paul's point on the call: it is low-margin service work that would need around two people at 60% of their time to run efficiently. Winning more of it before that team exists would make things worse, not better. Turned on later.

October 2026

Wind turbines return

Three brands. The company installed wind from around 2008 until 2017 and stopped because the early bladed turbines kept failing in this climate. Coming back now the technology is reliable. Height is limited to 25 feet inside city limits, so this is aimed at rural and acreage properties.

January 2027

Rebrand

To All Cities, Solar, Wind and Power, reflecting all three lines of work.

What This Means For Whoever Handles Enquiries

This is the part that affects your day

Today an enquiry arrives as a phone call or an email with nothing attached, and someone has to spend a call working out whether it is real. Roughly half of what comes through the website is people selling something.

After the build, an enquiry will arrive with answers already attached — whether they own the property, roughly what their power bill is, how old the roof is, which county they are in, and often a copy of their bill. You will know before you pick up the phone whether it is worth a site visit.

Three things that will be asked of the team:

  • Speed of response. Advertising is paid for per click. An enquiry that waits until Monday is money already spent, and in this market the first company to call back usually wins the job.
  • Marking what happened. Whether an enquiry turned into a site visit, a quote or a job. Without that, there is no way to tell which campaigns produce real work and the advertising cannot improve.
  • Asking for reviews. After every completed job. It costs nothing, and it is the single biggest thing that decides which company a homeowner calls first.

None of this adds admin for its own sake. The whole point is fewer wasted calls, not more paperwork.
