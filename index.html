So a homeowner in Yakima reads one number and, if they tap rather than type, dials another. Some of those calls will connect somewhere you are not, and you will never know they happened. In a year where every enquiry counts, this is the most expensive small thing on your website.

And there is no form

We checked every page. There is no enquiry form anywhere on allcitiessolar.net. "FREE quote" appears as a call to action, but the only route to you is the phone.

Solar is researched in the evening. Someone comparing quotes at nine at night cannot do anything with your site except write your number down and intend to ring tomorrow, which many of them will not do.

Your market, and why this year is not your fault

You said the tax credit removal hurt. Here is the size of it, verified.

What actually changed

The One Big Beautiful Bill was signed on 4 July 2025. It terminated Section 25D — the 30% residential clean energy credit — for any system placed in service after 31 December 2025. Not reduced. Ended. There is no residential federal credit in 2026.

Two effects landed together. Every residential quote you write in 2026 is effectively 30% more expensive to the customer than the identical quote in December. And Q4 2025 was a nationwide scramble to get systems installed and utility permission to operate before the deadline — so this year's demand was partly spent in advance.

This is industry-wide. You were not outcompeted.

What that means for the conversation. There is no marketing that restores a 30% price cut. What marketing can do is win a larger share of the enquiries that still exist, and make sure none of them are lost to a wrong phone number or a missing form.

The question worth answering on the call

Commercial solar did not end the same way. The residential 25D credit is gone, but the commercial-side credits ran on different rules and timelines. You already sell commercial solar, battery storage and electrical work.

That may be the only part of your business with a federal incentive still attached. It is worth knowing what share of your revenue it is and whether it can be grown, before anyone spends money advertising residential.

Confirm the current commercial position with your accountant — incentive rules have moved fast and we would not want you acting on our summary of them.

One fact worth putting on your website

Yakima gets over 152 sunny days a year and averages 6 to 7 hours of usable sunlight a day. That is genuinely strong solar resource — better than most of Washington, and better than a lot of places where solar sells well.

With the federal credit gone, the argument shifts from "there's 30% off" to "your roof actually produces". That number is your argument and it is nowhere on your site. Confirm the current figure before publishing it.

Estimated size, and what a normal year looks like

Estimate

A normal year: revenue roughly $2 million to $3 million, net profit roughly $150,000 to $250,000.

How we got there. Public records list nine employees. US residential solar installers run roughly $200,000 to $350,000 of revenue per head. Eastern Washington systems typically run 8 to 12 kW at around $3.00 to $3.50 per watt installed, so $25,000 to $40,000 per residential job, and a two-crew operation completing two to three installs a week across a working season lands in the same band by a second route.

Small solar installers typically run 5 to 10 percent net.

And 2026 is not a normal year

With residential demand down sharply across the industry, a realistic 2026 sits closer to $1 million to $1.8 million, with profit at or near break-even — because nine people is a fixed cost and the revenue halved underneath it.

That is the honest picture, and it is the reason the free fixes in this document come first.

Priority order

The first three are free and take under an hour between them.

  1. Fix the tap-to-call number

    Ten minutes in Squarespace. Every day it stays wrong, some proportion of your enquiries ring a number you are not answering.

  2. Answer the BBB complaint

    A B− with revoked accreditation for not responding is a fixable problem, and solar buyers check BBB more than almost any other trade.

  3. Put a form on the site

    Property type, roof age, average power bill, and whether they own the home. Solar gets researched at night and you currently have no way to catch it.

  4. Get current reviews onto Google

    Your visible reviews stopped in 2019 under a different phone number. Recent reviews under the current business are the fastest credibility you can build.

  5. Lead with financing, not the credit

    The federal credit is gone and every competitor's page still talks about it. A page built for the homeowner asking "does this still make sense without the 30%?" would be the only honest one in your market.

  6. Publish licence, certification and insurance

    Licence number, NABCEP if held, and insurance. Buyers are told to check all three and yours are currently invisible.
