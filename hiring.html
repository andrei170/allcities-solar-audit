You Are Not Short Of 02s. You Are Short Of People Who Have Been Given 4,000 Hours.

4,000
hours to an 02 Residential certificate — and you can supply them

Every solar firm in the valley is bidding for the same finished electricians. You are a licensed electrical contractor, which means you can do something they mostly cannot.

You can register electrical trainees and accrue their hours yourself. That turns a hiring problem into a training pipeline, and it turns your job advert into an offer nobody else in Yakima is making.

The Insight — 4,000 Hours Is About Two Years, And You Own The Clock
Why this changes your recruitment pitch entirely
  • 4,000 hours is roughly two years of full-time work. An 02 does not appear from nowhere; somebody supervised them for two years.
  • You are a licensed electrical and general contractor. Most of the outfits you compete with for labour are not, which means they can only hire finished electricians. You can create them.
  • 75% supervision on a 4,000-hour specialty is workable at your crew size. Pairing a trainee with a certified electrician is close to how you already run a two-man install crew.
  • And it changes the advert. "Electrician wanted" competes with every firm in the valley. "Come and get your 02 with us — we will supervise your hours" competes with almost nobody.
The honest cost of it

A trainee is not free labour — you are paying somebody while they learn and tying up a certified electrician's time to supervise. The return arrives in year two, which is exactly the horizon you described when you talked about going from two crews to four.

The alternative is bidding against everyone else for a small pool of finished 02s, which is what you are currently doing and why it is hard.

Where To Actually Look — And It Is Not Other Solar Firms
Yakima has adjacent certificate holders nobody is recruiting
  • (03) Pump and Irrigation is also a 4,000-hour certificate, and Yakima Valley is orchards, hops and irrigation. There are 03 holders in this valley whose work is seasonal and weather-dependent, who already understand conduit, trenching, three-phase and agricultural service. That is a shorter jump to solar than most people assume.
  • (06) Limited Energy and (06A) HVAC/Refrigeration — also 4,000 hours, also local, also used to working on roofs and in crawl spaces.
  • (07) Nonresidential Maintenance — people already doing commercial service work, which is the direction you said you want to grow.

Every solar company in the state advertises for solar installers. Almost nobody advertises to an irrigation electrician whose season ends in October.

Treat these as a range to decide within, not as a benchmark

These are job-board aggregator figures, not government wage data, and they disagree with each other by more than $10 an hour. We are showing you the spread precisely because it is wide.

The number that matters is the one you put in the advert. Most local adverts say "competitive pay", and the ones that state a figure consistently get more applicants worth interviewing.

The Plan, In Four Steps
  • Confirm the trainee route with L&I — registration, supervision ratio and the classroom requirement, in writing, for your licence. Everything below depends on it.
  • Decide your number. The local range runs from $23 to $55 an hour depending on certificate and experience. Pick where a trainee starts and where an 02 lands, and say both out loud.
  • Advertise the certificate, not the job. "Get your 02 with us" to people already in the trades, and target the adjacent certificates — irrigation, limited energy, HVAC — rather than competing for finished solar installers.
  • Build a page that sorts them before they reach you — certificate held, hours already accrued, driver's licence, comfortable on a roof, when they can start. You are trying to add roughly eleven people; you do not want eleven times the phone calls.

Two crews to four is not a recruitment problem. It is a two-year supply problem.

You hold the licence that lets you solve it. Almost nobody you are bidding against does.
