Finding 1 — Your Most Important Page Is Unfinished
Live right now at /government-incentives
Page allcitiessolar.net/government-incentives Content "It all begins with an idea. Maybe you want to launch a business. Maybe you want to turn a hobby into something more..." Meaning unedited Squarespace placeholder text

That is template filler, published live, on the page a customer opens when they want to know what the government still pays for. Santiago tells us the removal of the 30% credit has hurt you. This is the page that should be answering exactly that, and instead it is telling people about launching a hobby business.

Here is why this is an opportunity rather than just an error. Every solar customer in Washington right now is confused about what changed. Most of your competitors have not updated their sites either — they are still advertising a credit that no longer applies to a cash purchase. The first company in Yakima to explain the new position clearly and honestly wins the customers who are currently too confused to buy from anyone.

That page could be the best lead generator you have, and today it is the worst page on the site.

Finding 2 — There Is No Form On Any Page, Including Contact Us
Checked across the homepage, contact, residential and commercial pages
Homepage 0 forms Contact Us 0 forms Residential 0 forms Commercial 0 forms Way to enquire phone only — (509) 248-2530

Your Contact Us page asks people to send their annual usage, address and account name — and gives them nowhere to put it. That instruction is exactly right; a bill gets you a real quote instead of a guess. But without a form and an upload, the customer has to work out how to send it, which most will not do on a Saturday evening.

You are also open by appointment only at weekends, which is when homeowners research solar. Everyone who lands on the site outside working hours currently has one option: remember to call you on Monday.

We are flagging this to verify rather than asserting it. Squarespace forms sometimes load after the page renders and would not appear in the source we read. Open your own contact page on your phone — if a form is there, tell us and we will correct this.

Finding 3 — Analytics Yes, Conversion Tracking No
Read from the live page source
Google Analytics 4 present — G-7DWRQN9VKJ Google Ads tag not present Google Tag Manager not present Meta pixel not present Call tracking not present

Credit where it is due — GA4 is installed and working. Most contractors we audit have nothing at all. It will tell you how many people visited and which pages they read.

What it cannot tell you is which of them became a customer, because there is no completed action on the site to count and no call tracking on the phone number. In a business where the enquiry is a phone call, that is the whole picture missing.

This matters more than usual for you right now. If the credit change has cut your enquiries, the only way to work out whether the problem is traffic, or conversion, or the market itself, is to measure each one separately. At the moment they are indistinguishable.

Finding 4 — You Tried Wind In 2007 And Rejected It. Ask Why Before April.
From your own Our Story page

Your history page says that in 2007 you looked at both solar and wind, and that “because of code limitations with Wind, we found Solar to have broader application in the Yakima area.”

Santiago tells us you are planning to expand into wind next April. That may be exactly right — codes change, turbine technology has moved on considerably in nineteen years, and the economics are different now that the residential solar credit has gone.

But it is worth being certain the thing that blocked it in 2007 has actually changed, because permitting is a county-level question in Washington and it is the sort of thing that stops a launch dead in month two. We are a marketing company and cannot advise you on any of that — but if the answer is yes, then the story writes itself: the family that has been doing alternative energy in Yakima since before most of your competitors existed, coming back to wind now that it finally works.

Finding 5 — Forty-Seven Years Of History, Almost Invisible
The best story we have read in a contractor audit this month

Your Our Story page traces the business from moving to Yakima in 1979, through cable television installation, a retail store in the old windmill building on South 3rd, satellite dishes, video rental, car audio, biodiesel classes in 2006, and into solar in 2007.

That is forty-seven years of the same family adopting whatever the next technology was, in the same town, before anyone else did. It is a genuinely unusual story and it is buried on a page most visitors never reach.

Homepage says "Clean energy solutions ... since 1979" Story page has the whole 47-year arc, in your own words Visitors who reach it: very few — it is not the argument on the homepage

In solar specifically this is worth more than in almost any other trade. The industry's reputation problem is companies that appear, sell hard, and are gone before the warranty matters. “Same family, same town, since 1979” is the answer to the objection every solar customer has and most are too polite to say out loud.

What Is Genuinely Good
There is a lot more here than the grade suggests
  • Since 1979, family owned, in Yakima. In an industry with a trust problem, this is the single most valuable asset you have.
  • Fifteen pages, including separate residential and commercial sections, battery storage, a gallery, FAQs and a store. That is a real website, not a brochure.
  • GA4 installed and working. More than most of your competitors have.
  • Named counties — Yakima, Kittitas, Klickitat, Benton and Franklin. Specific, and right for local search.
  • A genuinely good meta description naming the services and the counties. It is over-stuffed with keywords, but the instinct is correct.
  • EG4 equipment pages and battery storage. With the residential credit gone, batteries and self-consumption are where the remaining argument lives.
  • Electrical work alongside solar. A real differentiator and a second revenue line most solar firms have to subcontract.

This is not a company that needs rebuilding. It needs its best assets — the history, the electrical licence, the batteries — brought to the front, and one page finished.
